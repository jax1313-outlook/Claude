## 1. Core Doctrine

Portal equals Presentation Layer.

The Portal is not optional. The Portal is how Dispatch becomes visible, usable, and valuable to humans.

Without Portal:

- Mike cannot see what matters.
- Drivers cannot receive useful operational information.
- Customers, brokers, or shippers cannot see confidence-building visibility.
- The system cannot deliver decision-ready output.
- Dispatch becomes hidden machinery with no human value.

## 2. Purpose

The Portal transforms Dispatch activity into human-usable deliverables.

The Portal should show the right information to the right user at the right level of detail.

The Portal does not create authority. The Portal displays information, collects approved human actions, and presents clear decisions.

## 3. Portal Views

### 3.1 Mike / Owner View

The Mike view is the command cockpit.

It should include:

- Decision cards
- Review cards
- Active work queue
- Packet approval items
- Load or opportunity recommendations
- Exceptions and conflicts
- Archive and Library review prompts
- High-value operational alerts

### 3.2 Driver Portal

The Driver Portal supports daily moment-to-moment operations.

It may include:

- Assignment information
- Route notes
- Pickup and delivery details
- Required documents
- Proof photo instructions
- POD package guidance
- Route risk notes
- Check-in prompts

### 3.3 Customer / Broker / Shipper Visibility

External visibility views may provide confidence-building windows, not internal system access.

They may show:

- Load status
- Proof package status
- ETA notes
- Delivery confirmation
- Document availability

External users must never receive direct access to internal databases, proprietary intelligence, raw scoring logic, or internal decision notes.

## 4. Portal and Cognitive Load

The Portal exists to reduce cognitive load.

The Portal must not become a warning wall.

Portal presentation should be filtered by consequence:

| Level | Display Type | Human Burden |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Silent log | None |
| 1 | Status | Awareness only |
| 2 | Review | Optional inspection |
| 3 | Decision | Mike action required |
| 4 | Conflict | Mike resolution required |
| 5 | Authority | Final approval required |

## 5. Portal Relationship to Dispatch Spine

The Dispatch Spine handles state, routing, validation, queues, storage, and audit trail.

The Portal displays the useful result of that machinery.

The Portal is the window. The Spine is the machinery. Manager is the office-control function. Mike is the authority.

## 6. Portal Relationship to Manager

Manager decides what deserves attention. Portal presents what Manager and the Dispatch Spine determine should be visible.

Portal should not force Mike to manually route routine work.

## 7. Portal Relationship to Publisher and Intelligence

Publisher produces human-facing draft deliverables.

Intelligence produces human-facing analysis and recommendations.

Portal presents those outputs clearly for review, approval, rejection, or revision.

## 8. Forbidden Portal Behavior

Portal must never:

- Approve work automatically
- Hide material risk
- Expose internal proprietary data to external users
- Allow external database queries
- Allow uncontrolled customer, broker, or driver access
- Replace Mike's final authority
- Display noisy low-value alerts by default

## 9. Success Standard

Portal succeeds when Mike can see what matters quickly and act with confidence.

Portal fails when the work is technically completed but not visible, usable, understandable, or decision-ready.
